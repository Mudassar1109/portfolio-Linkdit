Technical SEO Checklist: Help Google Discover and Index Your Pages

Understand crawlability, internal links, canonical URLs, sitemaps and Search Console checks for small business websites.

By Muhammad Mudassar Elahi · Updated November 20, 2026

Publishing a page does not mean Google has discovered, crawled, indexed or ranked it. These are separate stages. Technical SEO makes it easier for search engines to access your important pages and understand how they fit into your website.

1. Discovery is not ranking

A URL can be discoverable but not indexed; an indexed page can still rank poorly. Check the URL Inspection tool in Search Console to see Google's reported indexing status and request indexing after meaningful updates. Requesting indexing does not guarantee inclusion or a particular ranking.

2. Make important pages crawlable

Use standard anchor links with real href attributes, connect related pages through descriptive internal links, avoid orphan pages and ensure navigation exposes key services and articles. Google recommends logical site organization and descriptive URLs.

3. Sitemap and canonical basics

Keep an XML sitemap containing canonical URLs you want indexed. Use consistent internal links, redirects and canonical tags to reduce duplicate URL confusion. A sitemap is a discovery hint, not a command that forces indexing.

4. A monthly technical review

Check Search Console indexing reports, sitemap status, manual actions and security issues. Test key templates on mobile, check broken internal links and review page speed. Fix errors based on impact instead of chasing every low-priority warning.

5. Diagnose an indexing issue systematically

Inspect the exact URL in Search Console, check the user-declared and Google-selected canonical, review robots directives and confirm the page returns the expected HTTP status. Then check whether important content and links are available without requiring an interaction Google cannot perform.

6. Build a crawlable site structure

Link priority pages from relevant navigation and supporting articles, use descriptive anchor text and avoid orphan URLs. Keep redirects, canonical tags and sitemap entries consistent. A sitemap helps discovery but does not guarantee indexing or rankings.

What the evidence says: Google explains that internal links help it understand site relationships and find pages; descriptive URLs and helpful content improve clarity.

Frequently asked questions

Does submitting a sitemap guarantee indexing?

No. It helps discovery but Google decides whether and when a URL is indexed.

How long does indexing take?

It varies. Google does not guarantee a fixed indexing timeline.
